TUTOR: Thanks, Ron, for coming to my office to discuss the presentation you gave yesterday to the business management seminar. We'll go over what you did with the aim of helping you improve your next presentation.
RON: Sounds good.
TUTOR: Now, the topic was the just-in-time, or JIT, supply system used by a lot of manufacturers, first in Japan and then in the West, I think.
RON: Yes, but actually one thing I should have made clearer and talked about was the fact that the Americans actually pioneered the idea of just-in-time, though it wasn't called that. Henry Ford and his cars, in fact, at the turn of the 20th century...
TUTOR: Yes, that would have been a good addition. Now, before we go any further, I think I would suggest that you should have stopped and clearly defined just-in-time for your seminar colleagues. You said its aim was to reduce waste... But you never told us that JIT simply means waiting until the last moment to order new supplies as you need them.
RON: Yes, you're right. I should have done that.
TUTOR: Now, you talked a bit about standardising parts as a way of streamlining JIT. Where did you get your data from?
RON: Well, I tried an internet website... but it was too technical for me. So, in the end, I got my data from a textbook called Learning from Toyota.
TUTOR: ...And while you're at it, you might want to find out about cases where JIT might not be suitable. And that's in service industries like tourism or healthcare, where customers are paying for something that they can't actually see or count. Although JIT is fashionable today, some people would say it hasn't proved so successful in the service sector as it has with the manufacturing of goods that can be quantified.
RON: That would be interesting.
TUTOR: Now, unless you have any more questions, let's turn to a critique of the technical aspects of the presentation...
RON: Actually, I wasn't happy with my delivery. I sometimes seemed to talk fast and sometimes slow. I was on edge, I guess.
TUTOR: Yes, you were. And as well as that, your purpose wasn't very specific. In future, you need to lay out exactly what you're going to cover and why.
RON: And what about my transitions from one section to the next?
TUTOR: Well, you didn't link the sections, did you? You went too quickly from one to the next. It was all just too abrupt.
RON: Okay, I'll try to improve that.
TUTOR: Now, your PowerPoint visuals were lovely, but sometimes I got the feeling that, nice as they were as pictures, they didn't have much to do with what you were talking about, right?
RON: You're right. And, you know, another thing, I could see from the other students' reactions, they just didn't understand some of the vocabulary I was using. Good point. Well, remember, your audience were laymen, mostly without knowledge of JIT. So, you need to simplify and try to put things in everyday English.
RON: I never seem to do that.
TUTOR: And lastly, I'll just mention your body language. You didn't use gestures and you just stood straight without any animation. It didn't make you seem very interested in the subject.

Section 4:

Dyscalculia, a complex neurodevelopmental condition, refers to a learning disorder marked by significant challenges in understanding and manipulating numerical concepts... Currently, most schools use the traditional method of teaching mathematics, which may not be effective for children with Dyscalculia.
These children struggle with grasping mathematical concepts, especially when it comes to understanding the concept of time and sequence.
In the classroom, teachers focus on providing everyday practical examples... They engage the students in activities that involve dealing with money, as this is a common challenge...
To boost their confidence, children with mathematics disorders are encouraged to practise more calculations and problem-solving.
Teachers also implement strategies that encourage children to make educated guesses when they encounter mathematical problems.
Additionally, they incorporate various exercises, such as breathing techniques, relaxation exercises, and even walking breaks...
Researchers in this field are particularly interested in finding a potential link between Dyscalculia and language learning difficulties.
Children with Dyscalculia often struggle with concentration and maintaining focus.
With this comprehensive approach to teaching children with Dyscalculia... children may no longer require additional treatment or training specifically focused on their mathematics disorder.
It's important to note that Dyscalculia affects not only the children, but also their families and teachers.
